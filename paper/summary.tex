\textbf{Abstract.}\;
Audio--visual LLMs routinely answer questions about \emph{sound} from \emph{visual} evidence: muting the
audio track of Qwen3-Omni leaves its answers to audio-existence questions unchanged, driving accuracy
from $95.1\%$ to $0.0\%$. We ask whether this can be repaired \emph{without touching the backbone}. We
attach a variational information bottleneck to each modality stream ($<\!0.5\%$ of parameters,
zero-initialised so that it is exactly the identity map at attachment) and train it with an
\emph{anchored} preference objective on constructed audio--visual conflicts. Standard DPO on these
pairs collapses to a constant response (perception accuracy $0.95\!\to\!0.10$); two explicit
constraints --- a chosen-answer anchor and a KL tether to the frozen backbone on ordinary inputs ---
remove the degenerate optimum. On AVHBench ($n\!=\!5302$) the adapter improves overall accuracy by
$+5.1$ points, and a query-conditioned (FiLM) variant by $+5.8$ ($p<10^{-4}$, paired McNemar), with
hallucination resistance preserved and non-significant changes on MMAU and Video-MME. Two probes
separate the mechanisms: both variants raise the answer's dependence on the audio by
$3.3$--$3.6\times$, but only the query-conditioned one also increases attention to audio--visual
tokens ($5.4\%\!\to\!7.4\%$). The method's reach is bounded by what the frozen backbone already
encodes: the gain is $+5.8$ on Qwen3-Omni, $+0.1$ on Qwen2.5-Omni and $-2.3$ on VideoLLaMA2, and on
temporal alignment --- where the backbone is at chance ($0.495$) --- no adapter helps.

\section{The problem: sound answered from sight}

Audio--visual LLMs encode each modality into tokens (audio $\sim$25/s; video sampled at 2\,fps in all
our Qwen3-Omni runs), project them into the LLM's embedding space and answer over the mixed sequence,
where text, audio and vision compete for attention. Audio consistently loses. \textbf{AVHBench}
(Sung-Bin et al., ICLR'25) shows cross-modal hallucination in every model it evaluates; \textbf{CMM}
(Leng et al., 2024) attributes it to unimodal priors, with audio the most frequently overridden
channel; \textbf{Wen et al.\ (2026)} characterise it as a \emph{Clever Hans} effect. Their audio
interventions on Qwen3-Omni --- \emph{our} backbone --- are decisive (accuracy \%):

\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l cc l@{}}
\toprule
probe & original & intervened & intervention \\
\midrule
audio existence   & 95.1  & \textcolor{red!70!black}{\textbf{0.0}}  & \emph{Mute}: silence the track \\
temporal sync     & 100.0 & \textcolor{red!70!black}{\textbf{1.4}}  & \emph{Shift}: audio moved $\pm2$\,s \\
sound consistency & 75.4  & \textcolor{red!70!black}{\textbf{37.3}} & \emph{Swap}: another clip's audio \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\cptn{Table 1.}{Reported values, arXiv:2605.16403, Tab.~1. Under muting accuracy falls to
\emph{zero}, not to chance: the answers stay consistent with the \emph{video}.}

Existing mitigations --- contrastive decoding (MAD), modality-decoupled DPO (MoD-DPO) and full
re-alignment --- all modify or bypass the backbone. \textbf{Our question: can audio grounding be
improved in a frozen backbone by training a parameter-efficient adapter alone?}

\section{Method}

\subsection{A variational bottleneck per stream}
The information bottleneck (Tishby et al., 2000) seeks a compressed $Z$ of the input $X$ retaining
only what predicts $Y$: $\max_{q(z|x)} I(Z;Y)-\beta_{kl}I(Z;X)$. Both terms are intractable and are
replaced by the variational bounds of Alemi et al.\ (ICLR'17); the compression term becomes the rate
$\mathbb E_x\,\mathrm{KL}(q(z|x)\,\|\,\mathcal N(0,I))$ and the encoder is trained by
reparameterisation. Concretely (Fig.~1b), per modality:
\[ z=\mu(x)+\sigma(x)\odot\epsilon,\qquad y=x+W_{\text{out}}z,\qquad W_{\text{out}}\stackrel{\text{init}}{=}0 .\]
Together the two modules are $<\!0.5\%$ of backbone parameters. Two properties matter: the module is
the \emph{identity at initialisation}, and bypassing it reproduces the frozen backbone bit-for-bit,
so the DPO reference model is free.

\subsection{Training data: constructed conflicts}
Substituting a clip's audio track yields an instance where the visible and audible events disagree
(\emph{see} a flute, \emph{hear} a train horn). Preferring the audio-consistent answer on such an
instance is only possible by using the audio stream.

\subsection{Why standard DPO fails}
With $c,r$ the log-probabilities of the chosen (heard) and rejected (seen) answer, subscript $p$ $=$
adapter on and $r$ $=$ bypassed,
\[
\mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}=-\log\sigma\big(\beta[(c_p-c_r)-(r_p-r_r)]\big),\quad \beta=0.1 ,
\]
where $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid, \emph{not} the encoder's $\sigma(x)$. This objective
constrains only the \emph{margin}: it is minimised even when both probabilities fall, provided the
rejected one falls faster, and the displaced mass accumulates on an unconstrained third response
(\emph{likelihood displacement}). Empirically the model converges to answering ``no'' everywhere and
perception accuracy drops from $\mathbf{0.95}$ to $\mathbf{0.10}$ --- while \emph{maximising} the
hallucination-resistance metric. A hallucination score alone is not a sufficient training signal.

\subsection{Anchored objective}
\vspace{-2pt}
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L =\ & \underbrace{-\log\sigma(\beta[(c_p{-}c_r){-}(r_p{-}r_r)])}_{\textbf{engine}:\ \text{prefer the heard answer}} \\[1pt]
 &+\ \lambda_a\underbrace{\big({-}\log\sigma(\beta(c_p{-}c_r){-}\delta)\big)}_{\textcolor{trn}{\textbf{rail 1}}:\ \text{chosen}\,\ge\,\text{base}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{PA}}
  \ +\ \lambda_{kl}\underbrace{\mathrm{KL}(p_{\text{base}}\|p_\theta)}_{\textcolor{trn}{\textbf{rail 2}}:\ \text{normal inputs}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{HR}} \\[1pt]
 &+\ \beta_{kl}\,\mathrm{KL}_{\text{VIB}}
\end{align*}
Rail 1 (an mDPO-style anchor) forbids the chosen response from losing probability relative to the
frozen backbone; rail 2 tethers the model to the backbone on ordinary, non-substituted inputs. The
uniform-``no'' solution violates both, so the preference term can only be reduced by \emph{using the
audio}.

\subsection{Query conditioning is necessary at the optimum}
Take one substituted clip and two questions over identical inputs --- ``what do you hear?''
(target: bell) and ``what do you see?'' (target: dog). With per-question loss $-\log\sigma(m)$
$=0.13,\,0.69,\,2.13$ at margins $m=+2,0,-2$:

\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l cc c@{}}
\toprule
adapter & HEAR & SEE & total \\
\midrule
cannot read $q$, commits to ``bell'' & $0.13$ & $2.13$ & $2.26$ \\
cannot read $q$, hedges 50/50        & $0.69$ & $0.69$ & $\mathbf{1.39}$ (floor) \\
\textbf{reads $q$} (FiLM)            & $0.13$ & $0.13$ & $\mathbf{0.26\!\to\!0}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\cptn{Table 2.}{A query-independent adapter has a loss floor of $2\log2\approx1.39$ per pair; a
query-conditioned one reaches zero. This is a property of the objective, not an empirical result.}

\section{Evaluation protocol}

\textbf{AVHBench} ($n\!=\!5302$ over 2{,}136 videos): A$\to$V $1{,}136$, V$\to$A $2{,}290$, A--V
Matching $1{,}876$; yes/no exactly balanced, so a constant answer scores $50\%$. V$\to$A --- is a
queried sound audible --- is our primary audio-grounding measure. \textbf{CMM} ($1{,}200$ samples
$\times\,2$ probes $=2{,}400$) reports perception accuracy PA (correct ``yes''/gold ``yes'') and
hallucination resistance HR (correct ``no''/gold ``no''); \emph{both must be read jointly}, since
answering ``no'' always gives $\text{HR}=1.0$, $\text{PA}=0$.

\begin{tl}\footnotesize
\item \textbf{Frame rate} fixed in code at 2\,fps for Qwen3-Omni (1\,fps for the 7B backbones),
identically for selection and final evaluation. Below 2\,fps Qwen3-Omni develops a systematic
yes-bias.
\item \textbf{Prompt sensitivity is large.} Changing only the answer-format suffix moved one score by
$+11$ points (V$\to$A, $69.0\!\to\!80.3$) at \emph{identical weights}. Prompts, suffix and decoding
are therefore fixed and logged.
\item \textbf{Checkpoint selection} on a validation half, results on the disjoint remainder. The
initially selected checkpoint did not survive this.
\item \textbf{Paired significance}: identical items for both models; McNemar with bootstrap CIs.
\item \textbf{Contamination}: training$\,\cap\,$benchmark clips $=\emptyset$, verified by script.
\end{tl}

\section{Mechanism: what the adapter actually changes}

Benchmark accuracy alone cannot distinguish two adapters that gain the same amount. Three probes do.

\textbf{(i) Attention to audio--visual tokens.} Share of the answer position's attention falling on
audio/video tokens (eager attention; comparable \emph{within} a model only). \textbf{(ii)
Audio dependence.} Substitute the audio and re-score the \emph{same} gold answer: a model that ignores
audio shifts by $\approx0$. \textbf{(iii) Parameters.} Read directly off the trained FiLM checkpoint,
with no inference at all.

\textbf{Reading.} Comparable accuracy gains are reached by \emph{two different mechanisms}: swap-DPO
raises audio dependence \emph{without} attending more to the evidence (it re-encodes the tokens),
whereas FiLM raises both. Parameter probes agree: the spectral norm ratio
$\|W_{\text{out}}\|/\|W_e\|\approx1.0$ (the edit \textbf{rotates} features rather than amplifying
them), the FiLM gate $g(q)$ \textbf{never falls below $0.4$} over the evaluation questions
(modulation, not routing --- it never switches a stream off), and question-driven modulation is
$\approx4.5\times$ stronger on vision than on audio, i.e.\ the query steers mostly by re-weighting
the \emph{visual} contribution. An earlier version of this analysis claimed that $59\%$ of the vision
stream was rewritten with the audio module inactive; direct parameter inspection did not support it
and the claim was withdrawn.

\section{Temporal alignment: a negative result that explains the rest}

The same objective applies with a different intervention: displace the audio by
$\Delta\in\pm\{0.5,1,2\}$\,s and prefer the correct synchronisation judgement. The preference term and
both rails are unchanged. It does not work, and the reason is instructive.

\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l c l@{}}
\toprule
AVE-Shift (held out, balanced $\Rightarrow$ chance $0.50$) & acc & reading \\
\midrule
base --- synchronised vs shifted ($n{=}200$) & 0.495 & at chance \\
base --- lag vs lead ($n{=}100$)             & 0.500 & at chance \\
base --- $\pm2$\,s displacement only ($n{=}200$) & 0.495 & at chance \\
\midrule
$+$ shift-DPO {\tiny(st.\ 60)} --- sync vs shifted & 0.515 & unchanged \\
$+$ shift-DPO {\tiny(st.\ 60)} --- lag vs lead     & 0.450 & unchanged \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\cptn{Table 8.}{Measured; all differences lie inside the $\pm7$-point interval at these sample sizes.
Row 3 restricts the probe to $\pm2$\,s --- four frames at 2\,fps, the largest and most clearly
resolvable offset --- and the backbone is at chance \emph{there too}.}

Training on displaced data leaves accuracy at chance, so the limitation is not the objective:
\textbf{the frozen backbone carries no usable temporal-alignment signal, and there is nothing for an
adapter to unlock.} This is the same pattern as audio on VideoLLaMA2, and together the two cases
state the law governing this method --- \emph{gain is proportional to usable-but-underused signal}.
This also reconciles our chance-level result with Table~1, where Wen et al.\ report a fall from
$100.0$ to $1.4$: a model that answers ``synchronised'' regardless of the input scores $\approx\!100$
on synchronised items and $\approx\!0$ on displaced ones, hence $\approx\!50$ on a balanced mix. The
two measurements are the same finding seen from either side, and $0.495$ is what our balanced probe
must report if theirs is right.

As a controlled test of the representability hypothesis we add a shared clock feature
$\tau_t=[\sin(2\pi t/T),\cos(2\pi t/T)]$ to both streams before the bottleneck,
$\tilde x_t=[x_t;\tau_t]$, so that misalignment becomes representable at all (audio at $t$ and a frame
at $t+\Delta$ then differ in encoding); $W_{\text{out}}$ stays zero-initialised. At 2\,fps the frame
interval of $0.5$\,s bounds the smallest resolvable displacement. \emph{Implemented and in training;
given the chance-level baseline we expect limited benefit and retain the run as a test of the
hypothesis, not as an expected improvement.}

\section{Limitations and status}

\begin{tl}\footnotesize
\item \textbf{Single seed.} All intervals reflect item sampling, not seed variance. Multi-seed
statistics, a $\beta_{kl}$ sweep, a VIB-ingredient ablation and a data-scaling curve are not yet
measured and are deliberately \emph{not} reported.
\item \textbf{In progress.} CMM for the cross-attention baseline; MiniCPM-O adapter training and
evaluation; the video-substitution and temporal-displacement variants are trained but not yet
evaluated.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Gains require a backbone with usable audio representations, and out-of-%
distribution audio reasoning shows a small, consistently negative cost.
\end{tl}

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Four model wrappers, five benchmark runners, four training variants, two
baselines and three analysis probes; ${\approx}10$k lines, 23 cluster job scripts, seven test suites.
Frame rate, prompts, answer format and decoding are pinned; selection is on a disjoint validation
half; per-item predictions are stored so every paired test can be recomputed. Each table and figure
is produced by a single script.

\section{Conclusion}

A parameter-efficient, zero-initialised variational adapter ($<\!0.5\%$ of parameters), trained with
an anchored preference objective on constructed audio--visual conflicts, improves audio grounding in a
\emph{frozen} audio--visual LLM by $+5.1$ points and, with query conditioning, $+5.8$ points on
AVHBench ($p<10^{-4}$, paired), preserving hallucination resistance and costing nothing significant on
MMAU or Video-MME. The improvement is supported by mechanistic evidence, not accuracy alone: audio
dependence rises $3.3$--$3.6\times$ in both variants, while only the query-conditioned one also
increases attention to the audio--visual evidence. Its limit is equally clear and equally measured ---
where the frozen representation carries no usable signal, whether that is audio on VideoLLaMA2 or
temporal alignment on Qwen3-Omni, no adapter can supply it.
