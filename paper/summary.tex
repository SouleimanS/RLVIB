\textbf{Abstract.}\;
Audio--visual LLMs routinely answer questions about \emph{sound} from \emph{visual} evidence: muting
the audio track of Qwen3-Omni leaves its answers to audio-existence questions unchanged, driving
accuracy from $95.1\%$ to $0.0\%$. We ask whether this can be repaired \emph{without touching the
backbone}. We attach a variational information bottleneck to each modality stream ($<\!0.5\%$ of
parameters, zero-initialised so that it is exactly the identity map at attachment) and train it with
an \emph{anchored} preference objective on constructed audio--visual conflicts. Standard DPO on these
pairs collapses to a constant response (perception accuracy $0.95\!\to\!0.10$); two explicit
constraints --- a chosen-answer anchor and a KL tether to the frozen backbone on ordinary inputs ---
remove the degenerate optimum. On AVHBench ($n\!=\!5302$) the adapter gains $+5.1$ points and a
query-conditioned (FiLM) variant $+5.8$ ($p<10^{-4}$, paired McNemar), with hallucination resistance
preserved and non-significant changes on MMAU and Video-MME. Two probes separate the mechanisms: both
variants raise the answer's dependence on the audio by $3.3$--$3.6\times$, but only the
query-conditioned one also increases attention to audio--visual tokens ($5.4\%\!\to\!7.4\%$). The
method's reach is bounded by what the frozen backbone already encodes: the gain is $+5.8$ on
Qwen3-Omni, $+0.1$ on Qwen2.5-Omni and $-2.3$ on VideoLLaMA2, and on temporal alignment --- where the
backbone is at chance ($0.495$) --- no adapter helps.
\end{minipage}

\section{The problem: sound answered from sight}

Audio--visual LLMs encode each modality into tokens (audio $\sim$25/s; video at 2\,fps in all our
Qwen3-Omni runs), project them into the LLM's embedding space and answer over the mixed sequence,
where text, audio and vision compete for attention. Audio consistently loses. \textbf{AVHBench}
(Sung-Bin et al., ICLR'25) finds cross-modal hallucination in every model it evaluates; \textbf{CMM}
(Leng et al., 2024) attributes it to unimodal priors, with audio the most frequently overridden
channel; \textbf{Wen et al.\ (2026)} call it a \emph{Clever Hans} effect. Their audio interventions on
Qwen3-Omni --- \emph{our} backbone --- are decisive (accuracy \%):

\begin{ctr}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l cc l@{}}
\toprule
probe & original & intervened & intervention \\
\midrule
audio existence   & 95.1  & \textcolor{red!70!black}{\textbf{0.0}}  & \emph{Mute}: silence the track \\
temporal sync     & 100.0 & \textcolor{red!70!black}{\textbf{1.4}}  & \emph{Shift}: audio moved $\pm2$\,s \\
sound consistency & 75.4  & \textcolor{red!70!black}{\textbf{37.3}} & \emph{Swap}: another clip's audio \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{ctr}
\cptn{Table 1.}{Reported, arXiv:2605.16403 Tab.~1. Under muting accuracy falls to \emph{zero}, not to
chance: the answers stay consistent with the \emph{video}.}

Mitigations --- contrastive decoding (MAD), modality-decoupled DPO (MoD-DPO), full re-alignment ---
all modify or bypass the backbone. \textbf{Our question: can audio grounding be improved in a frozen
backbone by training a parameter-efficient adapter alone?}

\section{Method}

\subsection{A variational bottleneck per stream}
The information bottleneck (Tishby et al., 2000) seeks a compressed $Z$ of the input $X$ retaining
only what predicts $Y$: $\max_{q(z|x)} I(Z;Y)-\beta_{kl}I(Z;X)$. Both terms are intractable and are
replaced by the variational bounds of Alemi et al.\ (ICLR'17); the compression term becomes the rate
$\mathbb E_x\,\mathrm{KL}(q(z|x)\,\|\,\mathcal N(0,I))$, and the encoder is trained by
reparameterisation. Per modality (Fig.~1b):
\[ z=\mu(x)+\sigma(x)\odot\epsilon,\quad y=x+W_{\text{out}}z,\quad W_{\text{out}}\stackrel{\text{init}}{=}0 .\]
The two modules are together $<\!0.5\%$ of backbone parameters. Two properties matter: the module is
the \emph{identity at initialisation}, and bypassing it reproduces the frozen backbone bit-for-bit, so
the DPO reference model is free.

\subsection{Training data: constructed conflicts}
Substituting a clip's audio track yields an instance where the visible and audible events disagree
(\emph{see} a flute, \emph{hear} a train horn). Preferring the audio-consistent answer there is only
possible by using the audio stream.

\subsection{Why standard DPO fails}
With $c,r$ the log-probabilities of the chosen (heard) and rejected (seen) answer, subscript $p$ $=$
adapter on and $r$ $=$ bypassed,
\[ \mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}=-\log\sigma\big(\beta[(c_p-c_r)-(r_p-r_r)]\big),\quad \beta=0.1 , \]
where $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid, \emph{not} the encoder's $\sigma(x)$. This constrains only the
\emph{margin}: it is minimised even when both probabilities fall, provided the rejected one falls
faster, and the displaced mass accumulates on an unconstrained third response (\emph{likelihood
displacement}). The model converges to answering ``no'' everywhere and perception accuracy drops from
$\mathbf{0.95}$ to $\mathbf{0.10}$ --- while \emph{maximising} the hallucination-resistance metric. A
hallucination score alone is not a sufficient training signal.

\subsection{Anchored objective}
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L =\ &\underbrace{-\log\sigma\big(\beta[(c_p{-}c_r){-}(r_p{-}r_r)]\big)}_{\text{\scriptsize \textbf{engine}: prefer the heard answer}}\\[1pt]
 &+\ \lambda_a\underbrace{\big({-}\log\sigma(\beta(c_p{-}c_r){-}\delta)\big)}_{\text{\scriptsize\color{trn}\textbf{rail 1}: chosen $\ge$ base $\Rightarrow$ PA}}\\[1pt]
 &+\ \lambda_{kl}\underbrace{\mathrm{KL}(p_{\text{base}}\|p_\theta)}_{\text{\scriptsize\color{trn}\textbf{rail 2}: normal inputs $\Rightarrow$ HR}}
   \ +\ \beta_{kl}\,\mathrm{KL}_{\text{VIB}}
\end{align*}
Rail 1 (an mDPO-style anchor) forbids the chosen response from losing probability against the frozen
backbone; rail 2 tethers the model to the backbone on ordinary, non-substituted inputs. The
uniform-``no'' solution violates both, so the preference term can only be reduced by \emph{using the
audio}.

\subsection{Query conditioning is necessary at the optimum}
Take one substituted clip and two questions over identical inputs, ``what do you hear?'' (bell) and
``what do you see?'' (dog). With per-question loss $-\log\sigma(m)=0.13,\,0.69,\,2.13$ at margins
$m=+2,0,-2$, an adapter that cannot read $q$ pays $0.13+2.13$ if it commits to ``bell'' and
$0.69+0.69$ if it hedges: no query-independent adapter beats that floor of $2\log2\approx1.39$ per
pair. One that reads $q$ pays $0.13+0.13$ and can drive it to zero. The data construction makes query
conditioning necessary \emph{at the optimum} --- a property of the objective, not an empirical result.

\section{Evaluation protocol}

\textbf{AVHBench} ($n\!=\!5302$ over 2{,}136 videos): A$\to$V $1{,}136$, V$\to$A $2{,}290$, A--V
Matching $1{,}876$; yes/no exactly balanced, so a constant answer scores $50\%$. V$\to$A --- is a
queried sound audible --- is our primary audio-grounding measure. \textbf{CMM} ($1{,}200$ samples
$\times\,2$ probes $=2{,}400$) reports perception accuracy PA and hallucination resistance HR, which
\emph{must be read jointly}: answering ``no'' always gives $\text{HR}=1.0$, $\text{PA}=0$.

\begin{tl}\scriptsize
\item \textbf{Frame rate} fixed in code at 2\,fps for Qwen3-Omni (1\,fps for the 7B backbones),
identically for selection and evaluation. Below 2\,fps Qwen3-Omni develops a systematic yes-bias.
\item \textbf{Prompt sensitivity is large.} Changing only the answer-format suffix moved one score by
$+11$ points (V$\to$A, $69.0\!\to\!80.3$) at \emph{identical weights}. Prompts, suffix and decoding are
fixed and logged.
\item \textbf{Checkpoint selection} on a validation half, results on the disjoint remainder. The
initially selected checkpoint did not survive this.
\item \textbf{Paired significance}: identical items for both models; McNemar with bootstrap CIs.
\textbf{Contamination}: training$\,\cap\,$benchmark clips $=\emptyset$, verified by script.
\end{tl}

\section{Mechanism: what the adapter changes}

Accuracy alone cannot distinguish two adapters that gain the same amount. Three probes do.
\textbf{(i)} The share of the answer position's attention falling on audio/video tokens (eager
attention; comparable \emph{within} a model only). \textbf{(ii)} Substituting the audio and re-scoring
the \emph{same} gold answer --- a model that ignores audio shifts by $\approx0$ (Fig.~3).
\textbf{(iii)} Reading the trained FiLM checkpoint directly, with no inference at all.

\section{Temporal alignment: a negative result that explains the rest}

The same objective applies with a different intervention: displace the audio by
$\Delta\in\pm\{0.5,1,2\}$\,s and prefer the correct synchronisation judgement, preference term and both
rails unchanged. It does not work, and the reason is instructive.

\cptn{Table 8.}{Measured; all differences lie inside the $\pm7$-point interval at these sample sizes.
Row 3 restricts the probe to $\pm2$\,s --- four frames at 2\,fps, the most clearly resolvable offset
--- and the backbone is at chance \emph{there too}.}

Training on displaced data leaves accuracy at chance: \textbf{the frozen backbone carries no usable
temporal-alignment signal, so there is nothing for an adapter to unlock} --- the same pattern as audio
on VideoLLaMA2, together stating the law behind this method: \emph{gain is proportional to
usable-but-underused signal}. It also reconciles our chance-level result with Table~1's
$100.0\!\to\!1.4$: a model that always answers ``synchronised'' scores $\approx100$ on synchronised
items and $\approx0$ on displaced ones, hence $\approx50$ overall --- one finding, either side, and
$0.495$ is what our balanced probe must show if theirs is right.

As a controlled test of representability we add a shared clock feature
$\tau_t=[\sin(2\pi t/T),\cos(2\pi t/T)]$ to both streams before the bottleneck ($W_{\text{out}}$ still
zero-init), so misalignment becomes representable at all; at 2\,fps the $0.5$\,s frame interval bounds
the smallest resolvable displacement. \emph{Implemented and training; given the chance-level baseline
this tests the hypothesis rather than promising an improvement.}

\section{Negative results, limitations, status}

\textbf{Six things that did not survive contact with data} (each informed the final design):
standard DPO on swap pairs collapsed to a constant response (PA $0.95\!\to\!0.10$), motivating the two
rails; GRPO on easy items gave zero advantage on ${\sim}2/3$ of steps (unanimous rewards carry no
gradient); the gate $g(q)$ tried as a router stayed $>0.4$ throughout (modulation beats routing); a
claim that $59\%$ of the vision stream was rewritten with audio inactive did not survive parameter
inspection and was withdrawn; LoRA at identical attach points reached $+2.8$ against FiLM's $+5.1$
with HR \emph{below} base (the zero-initialised residual form is what helps, not parameter count); and
attaching the bottleneck \emph{pre}-adapter gave comparable accuracy but worse portability (one
attachment width per backbone, post-adapter, versus one per modality per backbone, pre-adapter) ---
its numbers are not in the current results directory and so are not tabulated here.

\textbf{Single seed:} intervals reflect item sampling, not seed variance; a $\beta_{kl}$ sweep, a
VIB-ingredient ablation and a data-scaling curve are not yet measured. \textbf{In progress}: CMM for
the cross-attention baseline; MiniCPM-O adapter training; the video-substitution and
temporal-displacement variants are trained but not yet evaluated. \textbf{Reproducibility}: four
model wrappers, five benchmark runners, four training variants, three analysis probes,
${\approx}10$k lines, 23 cluster job scripts, seven test suites; frame rate, prompts, answer format and
decoding are pinned, selection is on a disjoint validation half, and per-item predictions are stored
so every paired test can be recomputed.

% ================================================================================================
\section{Conclusion}

A zero-initialised variational adapter ($<\!0.5\%$ of parameters) trained with an anchored preference
objective improves audio grounding in a \emph{frozen} audio--visual LLM by $+5.1$--$5.8$ AVHBench
points ($p<10^{-4}$), for free on capability, with mechanistic evidence to back the number. Its limit
is just as measured: where the frozen representation carries no usable signal --- audio on
VideoLLaMA2, timing on Qwen3-Omni --- no adapter can supply it.
